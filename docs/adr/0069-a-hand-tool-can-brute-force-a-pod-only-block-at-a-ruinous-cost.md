# A hand tool can brute-force a pod-only block, at a ruinous cost

Amended 2026-11-25 (#858): every hand tool breaks a pod-only block, in every layer, and nothing is refused for being too weak. A diamond or better tool pays half its durability, flat; any lesser tool pays all of it and is destroyed as the block goes. This replaces the per-layer tool tags and the 0.75, 0.9 and 1.0 shares below.

Amends SPEC section 9 (Hardness), [ADR 0050](0050-company-rock-can-be-blasted-and-the-drill-still-refuses-it.md) and [ADR 0052](0052-the-prospectors-best-drill-bites-company-rock-and-a-shaped-charge-clears-a-column.md). Issue #858. A player with a strong pickaxe broke breach crust and it "came back": the server cancelled the break, the client was not told, so it ghost-broke, and nothing said why. The user's rule: one intended way to play, but no hard block. A determined player can brute-force it, at a cost that makes it futile, and the game says what they are doing wrong.

## Decision

- **One rule, one place.** `HandBreak.isPodOnly(level, pos, state)` is true for the breach crust, Company rock (`deepcharter:undiggable`) and `deepcharter:deep_rock` where the block's layer is above 1. It replaces the three separate refusals (`BreachService`, `LayerRock`, `CompanyRock`). It lives in `layer/` because the layer owns the crust and the bands, and it reads only the level, so it works on the client. Creative players are exempt. Pod drills and charges remove blocks directly and never meet it.
- **Break time is constant.** For a non-creative player the destroy progress of a pod-only block is `1 / breakTicks` a tick (`breakTicks` 1200, 60 s) when the held item is a tool, and 0 otherwise. Efficiency, Haste, Mining Fatigue, water and footing are ignored.
- **The ghost-break fix.** A mixin on `BlockBehaviour.BlockStateBase.getDestroyProgress(Player, BlockGetter, BlockPos)`, at its head, returns that progress. The client's prediction (`MultiPlayerGameMode.continueDestroyBlock`) and the server's check (`ServerPlayerGameMode`) both read this method and it runs the same code on both sides, so the client never predicts a break the server refuses. A swing with no tool is refused on the client by an `AttackBlockCallback`, so no crack starts; the server's copy is the backstop.
- **Every tool bites, in every layer.** The block's layer is its band's layer at its Y; the crust counts as the layer whose floor it is, unbuilt depth of the campaign world as the layer below the last built one, and the surface as layer 1. The layer no longer changes the rule. A tool is an item in `deepcharter:hand_break/tools`: the pickaxes, axes, shovels, hoes, swords and spears (the vanilla item tags, so a pack extends them). A bare hand, a stick, shears, a fishing rod, an elytra or a block is no tool and makes no progress. A tool that cannot be damaged (Unbreakable) breaks the block and pays nothing. Nothing is refused for being too weak.
- **Two costs, by tier.** The item tag `deepcharter:hand_break/durable` lists the diamond and netherite tools (pickaxe, axe, shovel, hoe, sword, spear); a pack adds to it. A tool in it pays `round(durableShare * maxDamage)` for a block, `durableShare` 0.5, flat in every layer. Any other tool (wood, stone, iron, gold, copper) pays all of its remaining durability and is destroyed as the block goes.
- **How it is spent.** Over the first `breakTicks - 1` ticks of mining the tool is damaged each tick, all but one point of what it will pay; the break pays the rest. A durable tool follows an even schedule of `round(durableShare * maxDamage)` points. A lesser tool spends `(remaining - 1) / ticksLeft` a tick, so it reaches its last point on the last mining tick and the break takes it: it can never wear out mid-way and leave the block standing. A tool that cannot pay (a worn diamond pick) is capped at its last point while mining and is used up by the break, and the block still goes. A break that comes early (a short stop) pays the whole remainder at once.
- **Unbreaking cannot reduce it.** The tool is damaged with `ItemStack.setDamageValue`, never `hurtAndBreak`, the one path that runs the item-damage enchantment effects. Mending repairs from experience and never changes the cost. Tests pin both outcomes with Unbreaking III and Mending on the pick.
- **Nothing drops, but the events fire.** The server takes over `ServerPlayerGameMode.destroyBlock` for a pod-only block and removes it with no drop, so brute force is never an ore exploit. Breaking the crust opens the way down as a bore does. It calls `PlayerBlockBreakEvents.BEFORE` (a veto refuses the break, charges nothing, and sends the client the block back), then `playerWillDestroy`, the removal and `AFTER`. It skips no listener: (A) brute-forcing a gas pocket vents it like any hand break, so the hazard still bites, and protection mods can still veto. Every refusal sends the client the block's real state, so no refusal leaves a ghost break.
- **It suffers visibly.** While the player mines, the server sends electric-spark particles at the face and a grindstone sound on a cadence, and an action bar line, "Pod drill only: your pick is burning out" for a durable tool and "Pod drill only: this will destroy your pick" for a lesser one. The first swing at a pod-only block per session (not saved) says in chat that a pod drill is the way and a pick burns itself out on it; with an item that is no tool it says the item cannot do this at all.
- **The crosshair readout** (#859) reads "Pod drill only" on every pod-only block for a non-creative viewer, and "Pod drill only: your pick would burn out" when the viewer holds a durable tool, and "Pod drill only: this will destroy your pick" when they hold a lesser one (A). Company rock reads its own "Charges, or the best drill" to a viewer who holds no tool. The layer feature registers its hint first, so it wins over the ore feature's.
- **Who says what.** The server tells a tool the first-try line and keeps one set for each line, cleared on disconnect, so a swing with no tool never uses up the line for one with a tool. A swing with no tool is refused on the client, which the server never hears of, so the client says its own line once a session (`client/layer/HandBreakClient`), forgotten on disconnect.
- **Tuning** is one record, `HandBreakTuning` in `LayerTuning`: `breakTicks`, `durableShare`, the `durableTools` tag and the effect cadences.
- **Mixin targets** (the M2 rule): `BlockBehaviour.BlockStateBase.getDestroyProgress` at head, and `ServerPlayerGameMode` `tick` at tail (the cost and effects of each mining tick; no Fabric event reaches it), `handleBlockBreakAction` at head (a new swing restarts the count) and `destroyBlock(BlockPos)` at head (the paid break, no drop, with the BEFORE and AFTER events); and, on the client, `MultiPlayerGameMode.sameDestroyTarget(BlockPos)` at return: vanilla takes a held stack that differs in any component, damage included, for a new tool and restarts the block, so each point of wear the server sends would reset the crack. The mixin counts wear alone as the same tool for a pod-only block.

## Consequences

- The default build gets easier to brute-force: any pick, even a wooden one, passes Company rock in any layer. That is fine: it is gone for good, one pick a block for a lesser tool and half a diamond or netherite pick, far more than a blasting charge or a drill bite.
- The 60 s is a floor. Vanilla's own check lets a STOP through at 0.7 of the break time, so the server refuses a STOP that comes more than `stopSlackTicks` (20, 1 s: the integrated client was measured 5 ticks ahead of the server under load, and network jitter and lag spikes add more) short of `breakTicks`: nothing is charged and the client is sent the block back. A STOP within the slack breaks the block and pays the rest. Only a STOP from the client is held to the time; a direct `destroyBlock` call by another mod is not. The count restarts when the held stack changes, so a client cannot mine with one tool and finish with another.
- The three by-hand refusals are gone, and with them `LayerRock` and `CompanyRock`. `deepcharter:deep_rock` moved to `HandBreak.DEEP_ROCK`.

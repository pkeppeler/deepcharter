# Tier 1 of every pod part is a crafted unit, fitted by use like the scanner's

Extends [ADR 0048](0048-the-tier-1-scanner-is-a-crafted-unit-fitted-by-use.md) (the scanner unit) and builds on [ADR 0047](0047-the-surface-has-no-ore-the-bootstraps-metal-is-salvage-and-the-yard-is-a-biome.md) (the bootstrap's metal is salvage) and [ADR 0010](0010-pod-stats-are-a-modifier-pipeline.md) (parts move a pod's stats). Issue #536. The user decided the rule on 2026-10-12: every first-tier pod part is crafted, not bought, and tier 2 and up stay purchases. The recipes and costs are the implementer's, under the creative liberty of `designing-mechanics`.

## Decision

- **Twelve new items are crafted: `deepcharter:drill_unit`, `hull_unit`, `engine_unit`, `fuel_tank_unit`, `radiator_unit`, `cargo_bay_unit`, `lights_unit`, `spoil_hopper_unit`, `liner_unit`, `sounder_unit`, `brace_unit` and `heat_shield_unit`.** With the scanner's, there is one unit for every track. Each has a shaped recipe (`recipe/<track>_unit.json`). The first six (a Mole's stock tracks) take iron, copper and redstone from the salvage crates; the radiator also takes a block of copper (nine ingots, made at a bench). The six hazard answers (lights, hopper, brace, sounder, liner, heat shield) take mostly **ore from the pod's cargo** (Ironium, Bronzium, Silverium or Goldium; see the ore processor below) and a few copper ingots and a redstone, which the crates give beyond the iron the first six use. The costs are in [mechanics.md](../design/mechanics.md#crafted-first-tier-parts-536-a).
- **A unit is fitted by using it on the pod, as the scanner unit is.** `PartFitting` (in `upgrade/`, moved from `scanner/ScannerFitting` and generalised) serves all thirteen units: right-click a pod of the player's own charter, and the pod gets a tier 1 part of the unit's track, stamped for the charter and numbered from the track's serials, and the unit is used up. A new hull refills the hull and a new tank the tank, as a purchase does (`UpgradeTerminal.refill`, now package-visible). The refusals are the scanner's: a pod nobody owns, a pod of another charter, a player on no charter, a pod that already has a part of this track for this charter, and a world whose serials or charters this build cannot read. A refusal keeps the unit and changes nothing. The part is the same `part_<track>` item and the same `PodComponents.install` as a bought one, so a tier 2 purchase replaces it and drops it, and everything that reads a tier is unchanged. `UpgradeEvents.FITTED` fires for every track.
- **The upgrade terminal does not sell tier 1 of any track.** A request for tier 1 is refused (`UpgradeRefusal.CRAFTED`) before any money moves, and the screen shows the row as "crafted, not sold". Tier 1 prices are 0 in `UpgradeTuning`; tier 2 and up keep the prices they had. The one-tier tracks (spoil hopper, brace, heat shield) have nothing left to sell.
- **Tier 2 does not need the unit fitted, except for the scanner.** A charter that skips a unit can still buy tier 2 over the stock part, or over nothing, at the same price as before. The scanner keeps ADR 0048's rule (`ComponentTrack.needsFirstTier()`, true for the scanner alone): its tier 1 is the first map a Mole has.
- **Old worlds keep what they bought.** A pod's parts are an attachment whose shape does not change, so nothing is migrated: a bought tier 1 part is the same item and label as a fitted one, works as before, and is not replaced (a unit used on a pod that holds a part of the track for its charter is refused and kept). A charter that bought a tier 1 part before this change has paid for what is now crafted, and that is the whole cost of the change to it.
- **These ids are permanent** (a player's pack, chests and recipes name them): the twelve items above.

## Why the cost is minutes, and why the units cost what they do

A purchase cost money, which a run earns; a unit costs crates, a furnace, a bench and ore, which take play time. The test (`FirstTierPartsCostTest`) prices both in minutes at the pace of the run the part was priced against: a stock run of a Mole in layer 1 earns $115 in 71 seconds (the model's bore and climb, 26 seconds, and an assumed 45 seconds of walking, boarding, refuelling and selling), and a run of a Mole with tier 2 parts in layer 2 (where the liner, sounder and heat shield were priced) earns more in about the same time. A unit is priced so that making it takes about as long as earning its old price did: six to eight ingots for a $200 part, and nine copper ingots' worth more for the radiator. Ore counts at the run's rate, as the sales it forgoes: a $60 Bronzium is 0.6 of a minute of layer 1 runs. The first six together take 1.09 of the minutes that buying them took (the runs speed up as parts come in; the crafting does not), and each of the six hazard units takes 0.99 to 1.14 of its old price's minutes. The first full run of a stock Mole is unchanged, because no unit is needed for it: the opening to the first dig is the kit, and the units come after.

The hazard units take the ore of the layer where their hazard lives, which is the haul the pilot digs anyway: the lights, hopper and brace from Ironium and Bronzium of layer 1 (the dark, the lava ladder's first rung and the crust), the sounder from layer 1's Bronzium and Ironium, the liner and the heat shield from the Silverium, Bronzium and Goldium of layer 2's upper levels. A test pins that each ore lies in the zone of its hazard and is in the expected haul of at most 8 runs of it (the heat shield's Goldium, about 7, is the slowest).

## The ore processor withdraws ore

Ore otherwise leaves a pod only as money, and a layer 2 block cannot be broken by hand (SPEC section 9, `LayerRock`), so a hand-mined source would put the liner's and the heat shield's ore out of reach and ask the pilot to leave the pod for a chore. Instead the ore processor gets one more action beside MAKE SLAG BRICK ([#363](../design/mechanics.md#liner-bricks-363-a)): **WITHDRAW ORE** (`deepcharter:withdraw_ore`). The pilot picks an ore and a count from the cargo of the pods parked at the processor, and those items go into the pack instead of being sold.

- No money changes hands. A kept ore is the sale it forgoes, which is the parity model's price for it.
- It works on every pod the charter may use that is parked at the processor, and the server checks it: the ore must be a real ore, the count at least 1, a pod must be parked, its cargo must be readable and hold that ore, and the pack must have room. It takes the least of the count, the cargo and the room, and each refusal leaves everything as it was.
- It moves items out of an existing store (`PodCargo.take`), so no saved format changes.
- The processor's view carries the cargo's ore counts, so the screen lists what can be withdrawn (`ProcessorView`, wrapping the work orders).

## Considered Options

- **Leave the hazard answers bought.** Rejected by the user's decision: every first-tier part is crafted.
- **Require the unit before tier 2 on every track, as the scanner does.** Rejected. A Mole has a stock part, and a hazard answer's tier 2 is priced against its hazard; it would make every tier 2 purchase a harder default than main for a charter that skipped the unit.
- **Take salvage metal alone for the hazard units.** Rejected. The set would take far more of the 160 crates a survey holds. Ore costs no crate and puts each unit where its hazard is.
- **Take slag brick for the liner and the heat shield.** Rejected. A brick reaches the pilot's pack only after the pod's rack is full, and needs the hopper unit first; a circle in the first hour. The heat shield burns bricks when it runs, which is its own use for them.
- **Break the ore out of the rock by hand.** Rejected. Layer 2 rock cannot be broken by hand, so the liner's and the heat shield's ore could not be had, and a chore beside the shaft fights the pod fantasy. The withdrawal above takes the ore the pod already hauled.
- **Take ore out of the pod as an ingredient in the crafting grid.** Rejected. Ore leaves a pod only at a terminal; the processor's withdrawal is that terminal action.
- **One generic `part_unit` item with a component naming the track.** Rejected. Twelve items give twelve recipes, names and textures, and the item a player holds says what it is; the scanner's unit already set that pattern.
- **Keep a nonzero tier 1 price in the table, unused.** Rejected. A price the terminal never charges is a lie in the table; 0 is how ADR 0048 marks a crafted tier.

## Consequences

- A charter's kit before its first run is unchanged. The twelve units add about 30 iron, 32 copper (16 of them the radiator's) and 6 redstone to the kit's 30, 22 and 12, and $1,750 of ore. The crates are iron-bound, so a charter takes about 61 crates for all of it (90th percentile 69), against the 160 of a 12-chunk survey. A world that runs low on crates presses spoil into more ([ADR 0047](0047-the-surface-has-no-ore-the-bootstraps-metal-is-salvage-and-the-yard-is-a-biome.md)).
- No charter needs money for a first-tier part, so the first sales go to tier 2 sooner: a tier 2 drill, tank or bay is the first purchase, $500 each, three layer 1 runs of a Mole with the tier 1 drill, tank and bay ($235 a run).
- A hazard answer costs ore a pilot would have sold: the ore is the price in play, and the pilot who skips a unit keeps the sale.
- **The hazard answers cost more on the money path than on main.** Without a unit, the paid route is tier 2, since tier 1 is not sold: lights $125 becomes $375, sounder $400 becomes $1,000, liner $500 becomes $1,000, and the spoil hopper, brace and heat shield (one-tier tracks) have no money route at all. Keeping tier 1 crafted for every part is the user's decision. The withdrawal makes it fair: the unit's ore is the run's own haul, kept instead of sold, so the crafted route costs the sales forgone (0.99 to 1.14 of the minutes the old price took to earn) and needs no new chore.
- The radiator's first purchase is its tier 2, $1,250, four layer 2 runs (`EconomyAffordabilityTest`). The liner's and the sounder's tier 2 are $1,000 each.
- The dev command `/deepcharter pod ...` that mints a tier 1 scanner is unchanged.

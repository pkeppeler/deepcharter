# A seam is crossed by a swap to a client world built ahead, and the teleport with its fade is the fallback

Issue [#217](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/217), slice 1 in [#1159](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1159). Settles what [ADR 0085](0085-depth-is-one-axis-over-a-stack-of-tall-dimensions-and-the-dimension-machinery-stays.md) left open ("how seamless the seam must be, and whether the fade and the arrival pocket stay"). Builds on the seam of [ADR 0029](0029-the-campaign-is-one-tall-world-and-the-uncharted-chain-joins-through-seams.md), whose rejection of a fade as the plan still holds.

Amended 2026-11-25 (#1254): the last Decision bullet, on the mid-layer seam.

Amended 2026-11-25 (#1316): slice 1 streams a rise too. The "Nothing is sent to the client" bullet no longer says a rise lands as before.

## Decision

- **The target is a seamless swap.** The server streams the next world's arrival area ahead of the crossing, the client builds a second world from it, and at the seam it switches to that world without vanilla's respawn path. Position, velocity, rotation and interpolation carry over.
- **Today's teleport and fade are the fallback, not the plan.** `BreachService`'s crossing, its fading `BreachPayload` and its carved arrival pocket stay as the path a crossing takes when the swap cannot run: a rider's client has not accepted the pre-built world. An obstructed arrival does not force the fallback: the swap carves the pocket and sends it ahead (slice 3).
- **A refusal sends the whole vehicle through the fallback.** The swap is claimed for a vehicle and its riders together. If any rider's client refuses, the whole vehicle crosses by the teleport: it does not bounce back up, and it never splits, with some riders below and some above.
- **The arrival pocket is a safety net.** It is carved only on the fallback path and when the swap's arrival point is obstructed. It is no longer the normal way to arrive.
- **The server streams the arrival area first, in every case.** This is slice 1, and it ships on its own because it also removes the stall the fallback has today: a layer-dimension crossing no longer generates the chunks it lands in on the server thread.
  - **The ticket.** `deepcharter:seam_arrival` is a ticket type registered in `BuiltInRegistries.TICKET_TYPE`: it loads and simulates its chunks and keeps the dimension active, with no `PERSIST` flag, so a restart takes none and the approach starts again. It times out 100 ticks after its last refresh. It is taken with `ServerChunkCache.addTicketAndLoadWithRadius`, which schedules the chunks on the worker threads and returns a future, and refreshed every 20 ticks with `addTicketWithRadius`.
  - **The area.** Radius 4 around the chunk under the root vehicle: 81 chunks, at full status.
  - **Whose approach.** One approach per root vehicle carrying a player, keyed by the root's UUID and listing the riders. A pod with two riders is one approach, not two. The approach has no level or player reference.
  - **The trigger.** The local distance `d` above the level's floor, in blocks, so it needs no global depth ([#1113](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1113)). A vehicle streams when `d` is inside 160 blocks of the floor, or when `d` is inside the lead and its descent speed would cover the lead's distance in the time the design fall speed does. The lead is 0.32 blocks per row of the world below, rounded up: 656 blocks for 2048 rows, 1301 for 4064. The design fall speed is terminal speed, 3.9 blocks a tick (78 a second). The descent speed is read over the last 10 ticks, so a vehicle that sits or climbs streams only inside the 160 blocks.
  - **The last level, the campaign world and the surface.** A level with no level below it takes no ticket, and neither does the campaign world: its layers are bands of one level and nothing is crossed ([ADR 0061](0061-a-crossing-in-the-campaign-world-is-an-event-in-place-found-by-the-root-vehicles-y-with-the-crust-rows-as-hysteresis.md)). The surface floor is not a seam ([ADR 0029](0029-the-campaign-is-one-tall-world-and-the-uncharted-chain-joins-through-seams.md)).
  - **The release.** An approach ends when its vehicle leaves the zone, changes level, is removed or disconnects. The ticket is not removed: it lapses on its timeout, which keeps the arrival area through the swap. A re-centre of a live approach does remove the old centre's ticket. A crossing that finds the area not loaded still works, waits for the chunks on the server thread as before and logs how many of the 81 were loaded.
- **Nothing is sent to the client in slice 1.** The player sees no change. Slice 1 streams the descent, and since #1316 the rise: a vehicle within the floor margin of a level's ceiling, with a level above it in the chain, takes the same ticket on the chunk under it in that level, measured from the ceiling with the closing speed read as the rise. A rise still lands by the fallback crossing: its riders are sent nothing and no swap is prepared going up, so the vehicle never splits and the fade stays. The swap and the data ahead going up wait for their own issue. The level above layer 1 is the surface (the overworld). Right after a crossing the vehicle sits inside the opposite direction's margin: a descent lands within the floor margin of the new level's ceiling, and a rise within it of the new level's floor. So it at once takes the ticket for the level it just left and holds it while it stays inside that margin. Those chunks are usually still loaded from the crossing, so it is cheap, and it ends on leaving the zone, as a hover over a floor does. The campaign world crosses a band in place and loads nothing, so it needs no ticket.
- **The Sodium outcome.** Sodium needs no mixin of ours: its chunk tracker is per level and its renderer seeds from it when a level is set ([#1183](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1183)). The swap works under Sodium and under vanilla's renderer alike.
- **A mid-layer seam sits in a plain slab.** Decided by the user on 2026-11-25 (#1254). A seam at a layer boundary sits in the grained crust, as before. A seam inside a layer sits in a plain slab: a few blocks of ordinary rock, with none of the grained-crust rules. Building the slab is separate work. Slice 1 does not depend on it: the trigger reads the distance to the level's floor and nothing about what is there.

## Considered Options

- **(a) A seamless swap alone, with no fallback.** It is the target, but it cannot be the only path. It needs Sodium's per-level `RenderSectionManager` to hold a second world, and mixins on the respawn path (`ClientPacketListener`'s dimension change, the level renderer, sound and the player's interpolation). A crossing then costs about 0.6 MB of chunk data per rider at 256 rows (7.5 KB a column, [#185](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/185)), 4.8 MB at 2048 and 9.5 MB at 4064. Every client without the pre-built world, every refusal and every obstructed arrival would have no way through. Rejected as the sole path.
- **(b) The teleport with its fade as the plan.** Cheap and already built, but it is what ADR 0029 rejected: vanilla's respawn path rebuilds every mesh, stops all sound and freezes the player for about 1.2 seconds, at the speed of a fall of 78 blocks a second. Kept as the fallback only. That rejection still covers it.
- **(c) The arrival pocket as the way to arrive.** A carved room under the ceiling hides the stall, but it cuts the world the player lands in, and the carve needs the chunks loaded on the server thread. Kept as a safety net.
- **Sending the chunks to the client in slice 1.** It would let the client build ahead at once, but it ships that data (0.6 MB per rider at 256 rows, 9.5 MB at 4064) for a swap that does not exist yet, and it cannot be tested without the Sodium spike. Deferred to slice 2.
- **A fixed 600-block lead.** ADR 0029's figure holds for a 256-tall world only. At 0.32 blocks a row a 4064-tall world needs 1301 blocks, and a fixed lead is too short for it and wastes streaming on a short one. Rejected for a lead that scales with the height below.
- **A vanilla ticket type** (`PORTAL` or `ENDER_PEARL`). Their timeouts (300 and 40 ticks) are not ours, and portals and pearls share them: a refresh by one is a refresh of the other. A type of our own keeps the timeout in `LayerTuning` and the ticket findable in a test.

## Slice 2: data ahead

Issue [#1185](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1185). The server sends the arrival area to the riders whose clients have the channels, the client builds the world under the vehicle in the background, and the respawn into that dimension adopts it. The swap without a respawn is slice 3. A client that cannot adopt takes the teleport and the fade, as before.

- **Payloads** (`layer/`, one record each). `seam_prepare` (S2C): the dimension, its type, the obfuscated seed, the sea level, flat, debug and the centre chunk, taken from `ServerPlayer.createCommonSpawnInfo` so they are what the respawn will say. `seam_chunk` (S2C): the dimension and vanilla's own `ClientboundLevelChunkWithLightPacket`, built as `PlayerChunkSender` builds it. `seam_discard` (S2C): the dimension. `seam_missed` (C2S): the dimension. A rider is served only when its client announced `seam_chunk`; one without the mod gets nothing and crosses by the fallback.
- **What is sent.** `SeamSender` runs at the end of each server tick, after the approach is `ready()`. Per rider: a prepare, then the chunks of the area nearest the vehicle first (squared distance, then x, then z), until the tick's estimated bytes reach `seamBytesPerTick` (49 152; the estimate is the packet's block data and 2048 bytes a light array) and at least one a tick. A rider that moves two chunks gets the prepare again and only the chunks it does not hold. A chunk estimated over the payload limit less 64 KiB (for the heightmaps and block entities the estimate leaves out) is never sent. A rider whose approach ends or who leaves the level gets a discard; a disconnect and a server stop drop the state with none. Built payloads are shared by the riders of a tick.
- **Radius.** The ticket of radius 4 puts its outermost ring at level 33, short of block ticking, and `ChunkMap.getChunkToSend` gives only block-ticking chunks, so what is sendable is the inner radius 3: 49 chunks. Vanilla sends the ring after the crossing from the player's own tickets. `seamRadius` stays 4 and 49 are sent. A radius of 5 would generate 121 chunks (49% more) and send 81 (65% more bytes) to have the inner 7x7 meshed in place of the inner 5x5 under Sodium. It is one number to raise later, if pop-in at the edge shows.
- **The client** (`client/layer/SeamClient`). A prepare builds a `ClientLevel` that is not the shown one, with the listener's difficulty and chunk radius; a chunk goes into its chunk cache and its light is queued on it. The light runs with the listener's `level` set to the held level, since Sodium reads that field when light is applied. The held level is drained each client tick and none of its renderer-facing callbacks reaches the renderer, which it shares with the shown level: `ClientLevelSeamMixin` cancels `sendBlockUpdated`, `setBlocksDirty`, `setSectionDirtyWithNeighbors` and `setSectionRangeDirty`, and `ClientChunkCacheSeamMixin` cancels `onLightUpdate`, while the level is the held one and not the shown one. It is released on a discard, a disconnect, or a respawn into its dimension that does not adopt it.
- **The crossing.** `BreachService` runs the teleport inside `SeamSender.crossing`. On the server, `ChunkMap.markChunkPendingToSend(ServerPlayer, ChunkPos)` skips a chunk the rider was sent, unless the chunk changed since (`ServerChunkCache.blockChanged` marks it and its eight neighbours stale) or `LightSync` is settling its light, so the pocket and its neighbours are sent again by vanilla. The skipped chunks are kept for `seamMissedWindowTicks` (100). On the client, `ClientPacketListener.handleRespawn` claims the held level when the respawn describes the dimension, type, seed, sea level, flat and debug the prepare did and the player is not dying; its two `new` expressions then return the held level and its data, and the rest of the method runs as it does. A respawn that does not adopt into a dimension the server prepared sends `seam_missed`, and the server sends the skipped chunks again.
- **The fade.** Every fading crossing, the fallback included, holds black at the end of its black stretch until the client has seen the respawn into the new level and reports itself loaded again (which follows the section the player is in being meshed), so no level pops in under a clear screen. A respawn just before the payload counts as seen; one long before does not. The hold is capped at 200 ticks, ten seconds, which also ends it if no respawn ever comes. Vanilla's renderer and Sodium's both answer "loaded", so one signal serves both. `BreachFadeHoldClientTest` steps the fade with a chosen answer, since a slow load cannot be staged.
- **No forget suppression.** Vanilla sends no forget packet at a change of dimension: the player has been removed from the old level when `PlayerChunkSender.dropChunk` runs, and a removed player is not alive. `SeamDataAheadTest.aCrossingSendsNoForgetAfterTheRespawn` guards that: a forget after the respawn would name a position the adopted level shares with the old one.
- **Mixin targets.** Server: `ChunkMap.markChunkPendingToSend(ServerPlayer, ChunkPos)` at its head; `ServerChunkCache.blockChanged` at its head. Client: `ClientPacketListener.handleRespawn` (after `ensureRunningOnSameThread`, the two `new` expressions, and the return), accessors and invokers on `ClientPacketListener`'s `level`, `serverChunkRadius`, `serverSimulationDistance`, `applyLightData` and `enableChunkLight`, the four dirty-mark methods of `ClientLevel`, and `ClientChunkCache.onLightUpdate`. Each has `require 1`: a rename fails loudly.
- **Considered and rejected.** Meshing the held level off screen: it cannot be done without the renderer's own state for a level it does not show (the reasoning is in #1183). Sending a chunk again on every change: it churns without bound under a lava flow or a digging pod, where marking it stale and sending it at the crossing costs one send. A per-chunk acknowledgement: the connection delivers in order, so every chunk is applied before the respawn that follows it.

## Consequences

- **No player-visible change in slice 1.** The campaign world has no seam below it yet. In the layer dimensions, a crossing stops stalling on generation when the player has lingered near the floor.
- **Cost.** One area per approaching root vehicle, never more: the centre moves to the root's chunk only once the root is 2 chunks (Chebyshev) from it, and the old centre's ticket is removed at that moment, so a pod moving sideways at speed holds 81 chunks, not one area per chunk it crossed. N approaching roots cost N times 81 chunks. The ticket simulates its chunks, so mobs of the lower world tick under it. With today's short layers the trigger zone covers most of the level above: layer_1 is 192 tall and the floor margin is 160, so a vehicle streams over most of layer_1. That is accepted. The lead of layer_2 (256 rows, 82 blocks) is under the margin, so the speed branch of the trigger only matters for tall worlds.
- **Registry sync.** `deepcharter:seam_arrival` is a registry entry of a vanilla registry. Fabric's registry sync needs nothing from the mod beyond registering it in `init`: the entry is registered on a client and a server alike, in the same order, and the client never reads it. A client without the mod cannot join anyway, as with the mod's blocks.
- **Slice 3.** Slice 3 is the swap itself and the accept or refuse handshake, below. A refusal sends the whole vehicle through the fallback, as above.

## Slice 3: the swap

Issue [#1229](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1229). A crossing whose riders are all ready moves the vehicle to the level below with no respawn: the vehicle keeps its entity id, UUID and object, and the client keeps its player, its loaded state and its sound. The spike ([#1183](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1183)) found it feasible: entity ids come from one global counter, so an id is valid in every level, and `PersistentEntitySectionManager$Callback.onRemove` frees the UUID and ends tracking without destroying the entity, as vanilla does when it moves a `ServerPlayer` between levels.

- **Where it branches.** `BreachService.cross`, once the arrival is computed: if every `ServerPlayer` in the root vehicle's tree passes `SeamSender.readyToSwap`, the crossing is a swap. Otherwise it is the teleport, the fade and the slice 2 adoption, unchanged. Both run inside `SeamSender.crossing`. The decision is all or nothing, made in the crossing tick with no wait, so a vehicle never splits.
- **The arrival.** `(root.x, arrivalY, root.z)`, with the rotation kept and the velocity zeroed, as the fallback's teleport zeroes it: an arrival is a floor away, or the carve lays one, and a vehicle must not land at terminal speed. The arrival is free, and not carved, only if every carried entity's box there is clear of blocks, entities and liquid and the blocks directly under the root's box have a collision shape and no fluid. Otherwise the pocket is carved (`preparePocket`, which lays a floor over air or liquid), and each changed chunk is sent at once as `seam_chunk`, ignoring the byte budget (at most 9 chunks), built after the carve. The server does not wait for the light the carve queued: the light engine's futures are completed by the server thread, so a wait on one from that thread only ends at its timeout (measured: a `waitForPendingTasks` future was not done after 60 s). Each packet is built from the loaded chunk (`getChunkNow`, since the light hold keeps `getChunkToSend` empty until a later tick), and `LightSync` sends the chunk again once its light is settled. Every chunk is built and checked before any is sent. Those chunks leave the rider's `stale` set and are marked so `takePreSent` skips them even while `LightSync` is watching them, and `LightSync` does not offer them again at the end of its hold; a chunk that changes again is no longer marked. A rider with no seam, a chunk that is not loaded or too big to send, or a carved pocket outside the held area turns the whole crossing into the fallback, which teleports the vehicle into the pocket the carve already made. Carving wins over falling back: in solid layers a fallback on obstruction would mean almost never swapping.
- **Readiness.** `readyToSwap` holds for a rider that can receive `seam_swap`, whose client accepted the current generation with no refusal since, and that was sent every sendable chunk within `seamSwapRadius` of the centre, the arrival chunk among them.
- **The server sequence** (`SeamSwap.move`), in this order, each step for its reason:
  1. `seam_swap` goes to every rider first, ahead of any packet about the new level, as vanilla sends the respawn ahead of the chunk and entity packets.
  2. Each non-player in the tree is detached from the old level by its `EntityInLevelCallback.onRemove(CHANGED_DIMENSION)`, not `setRemoved`: `setRemoved` runs `stopRiding` on the passengers and sends `SetPassengers`.
  3. Each rider is removed by `ServerLevel.removePlayerImmediately` and `unsetRemoved` and sent the difficulty and permission level. The vanilla removal is needed: a callback-only detach would let `updatePlayerStatus(false)` send forgets that wipe the held level.
  4. `setLevel`, `setServerLevel` and the position, then `addDuringTeleport` into the new level, the vehicle first, then each rider after `connection.teleport` and `resetPosition`. Slice 2's skip applies, and the pod pairs at once.
  5. Follow-ups: level info, player info, post effects, `resetEmptyTime`, and Fabric's `ServerEntityLevelChangeEvents.AFTER_ENTITY_CHANGE_LEVEL` and `AFTER_PLAYER_CHANGE_LEVEL` fired by hand, since no respawn path fires them.
  
  Skipped, because the prepare already carried the level data and nothing is destroyed: the respawn packet, the ability and effect resends, `stopUsingItem` and the dimension-change triggers. `BreachPayload` gains a `swapped` flag, so a swap rumbles and does not fade.
- **The handshake.** `seam_prepare` carries a `generation`. Each prepare bumps the rider's generation and clears its `accepted` flag. The client answers `seam_accept(dimension, generation)` once the held level is built or kept for that prepare, or `seam_refuse(dimension, generation, reason)` with a pinned reason, `BUILD_FAILED`, `RELEASED` or `SWAP_FAILED`. An accept of an older generation is not an accept.
- **The client** (`SeamClient.swap`, one Fabric global receiver of `seam_swap`). It checks that the held level exists with the generation, the player is alive, the root vehicle's id and UUID match, every carried entity exists and the held level has the arrival chunk; a failure is recovery, below. Then it flushes the held level's light, moves each carried entity into the held level (`setLevel`, a shift of `pos` and `oldPos` by the move, the vehicle's interpolation cancelled, `addEntity`; never removed from the old level, since removal calls `unRide`), points the listener and `Minecraft` at the held level with the no-sound `updateLevelInEngines`, sets the camera, clears the held and prepared state, opens the carried window, and fires Fabric's `AFTER_CLIENT_LEVEL_CHANGE` and our `SeamSwapEvents.SWAPPED`. `PodLoops` re-keys its tracked level on `SWAPPED`, so the pod's loops do not cut. Not done: `SoundManager.stop`, a new `LocalPlayer`, `setClientLoaded(false)`, the loading screen, `MusicManager.stopPlaying`, `setLocalMode`, the debug subscriber reset.
- **The carried window**, `seamMissedWindowTicks` long after a swap. The server's remove packets for the old level name the carried ids and its add packets for the new level name them again: `handleRemoveEntities` drops the carried ids from the removal, and `handleAddEntity` of a carried id with the same UUID and type syncs the position codec and is cancelled. When the window ends, carried non-local entities that never paired are discarded.
- **Recovery.** A client that cannot swap sends `seam_refuse(SWAP_FAILED)` naming the generation. If the server swapped that generation within `seamMissedWindowTicks`, `SeamSwap.recover` runs for that rider alone: it sends the respawn packet and the vanilla follow-ups, then `removePlayerImmediately`, `unsetRemoved` and `addDuringTeleport` in the same level, and the rider stays mounted on the vehicle (the vehicle already moved with the swap). That client sees one loading screen and starts the fade while it waits. This is the one place a vehicle's riders are served differently, and the vehicle is never split: its position is the new level's for all of them.
- **Mixin targets, each `require 1`, with its 26.3 descriptor.** An injector or a wrapped call names its method with the descriptor; an accessor or an invoker takes the name of the member and matches the descriptor of the method it is declared with, so a rename or a changed signature fails the mixin at apply time in either case. Slice 3 adds:
  - `Entity` (accessor `levelCallback`, field `Lnet/minecraft/world/level/entity/EntityInLevelCallback;`; invoker `setLevel(Lnet/minecraft/world/level/Level;)V`; invoker `unsetRemoved()V`);
  - `ServerPlayer` (setter accessor of the field `isChangingDimension`, `Z`);
  - `Minecraft` (invoker `updateLevelInEngines(Lnet/minecraft/client/multiplayer/ClientLevel;Z)V`);
  - `ClientPacketListener` (setter accessor of the field `levelData`, `Lnet/minecraft/client/multiplayer/ClientLevel$ClientLevelData;`; injector at the head of `handleAddEntity(Lnet/minecraft/network/protocol/game/ClientboundAddEntityPacket;)V`, after `ensureRunningOnSameThread`; `@WrapOperation` of the `entityIds()Lit/unimi/dsi/fastutil/ints/IntList;` call of `ClientboundRemoveEntitiesPacket` in `handleRemoveEntities(Lnet/minecraft/network/protocol/game/ClientboundRemoveEntitiesPacket;)V`);
  - `ClientLevel` (invokers `getAllMapData()Ljava/util/Map;` and `addMapData(Ljava/util/Map;)V`, which vanilla's respawn uses to copy the map data into the new level).

  Slice 2's targets, for the same bump check: `ChunkMap.markChunkPendingToSend(Lnet/minecraft/server/level/ServerPlayer;Lnet/minecraft/world/level/ChunkPos;)V`; `ServerChunkCache.blockChanged(Lnet/minecraft/core/BlockPos;)V`; `PlayerChunkSender.sendChunk(Lnet/minecraft/server/network/ServerGamePacketListenerImpl;Lnet/minecraft/server/level/ServerLevel;Lnet/minecraft/world/level/chunk/LevelChunk;)V`; `ClientPacketListener.handleRespawn(Lnet/minecraft/network/protocol/game/ClientboundRespawnPacket;)V`; `ClientLevel.sendBlockUpdated(Lnet/minecraft/core/BlockPos;Lnet/minecraft/world/level/block/state/BlockState;Lnet/minecraft/world/level/block/state/BlockState;I)V`, `setBlocksDirty(Lnet/minecraft/core/BlockPos;Lnet/minecraft/world/level/block/state/BlockState;Lnet/minecraft/world/level/block/state/BlockState;)V`, `setSectionDirtyWithNeighbors(III)V` and `setSectionRangeDirty(IIIIII)V`; `ClientChunkCache.onLightUpdate(Lnet/minecraft/world/level/LightLayer;Lnet/minecraft/core/SectionPos;)V`; the listener's accessors `level`, `serverChunkRadius`, `serverSimulationDistance` and invokers `applyLightData(IILnet/minecraft/network/protocol/game/ClientboundLightUpdatePacketData;Z)V` and `enableChunkLight(Lnet/minecraft/world/level/chunk/LevelChunk;II)V`. Each of slice 2's injectors names its method with the descriptor too.
- **Tuning.** `LayerTuning.seamSwapRadius` (3, at most the send radius, `seamRadius` less 1): the chunks around the centre a rider must hold for the swap. `seamMissedWindowTicks` is reused for the carried window and the recovery.
- **The client's events.** `ClientLevelEvents.AFTER_CLIENT_LEVEL_CHANGE` fires at a swap, and then our `SeamSwapEvents.SWAPPED`, which says the level changed with the player, the vehicle and the sounds kept. `BreachEffects` is begun by the breach payload, and starts the fade when that payload follows a swap the client could not take.
- **Renderers.** Vanilla's renderer rebuilds every section from the held level when the level is set. Sodium runs `initRenderer` twice and seeds from the held level's chunk tracker, so it needs no mixin: `SeamSwapClientTest` passes with Sodium 0.9.2 on the client's classpath (a one-off run, not part of the build). Flawless Frames is optional: its tuning arrives with the Flawless Frames follow-up ([#1184](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1184)), after a measurement on the Mac.

### Considered options in slice 3

- **A light respawn, where the pod gets a new id (plan B).** It needs no entity surgery, but the pod's id changes, so every client reference to it (the riders' mount, pod loops, the camera) must be re-paired, and the respawn path's cost returns in part. Rejected while the same-id swap is feasible.
- **Moving the vehicle on the server with `create` and `setId`.** `ejectPassengers` sends dismount packets, which the riders would see as a dismount. Rejected.
- **Falling back when the arrival is obstructed.** Rejected for the carve, above.

### Consequences of slice 3

- **Particles** of the old level are cleared at the swap, as a respawn clears them.
- **Spectators** of a rider are not carried (`teleportSpectators` is skipped): a known gap.
- **Mods that hook `handleRespawn`** see no respawn. Fabric's level-change events are fired by hand to cover the ones that listen to them.
- **Pop-in** under vanilla's renderer is not measured here; it is the first thing to look at in the Mac playtest.
- **Bounded brittleness.** The swap leans on five private members of vanilla and a `Minecraft` method. Each target above fails at mixin apply (`require 1`) on a rename, not at run time.
- **The vehicle at rest after a swap or a recovery** is paired with its riders only when the chunk tracking is read again, which vanilla does when someone changes section. `SeamSwap.tick` reads it each tick for `seamMissedWindowTicks` after a swap or a recovery.
- **A failed swap** leaves the client between levels until the recovery respawn: between the failure and that respawn it may apply packets for the new level to the old one, and the respawn heals it.
- **A rider player that carries its own passenger** loses it in `removePlayerImmediately`. Pods do not seat one, so it is out of scope.

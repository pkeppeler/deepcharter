# A seam is crossed by a swap to a client world built ahead, and the teleport with its fade is the fallback

Issue [#217](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/217), slice 1 in [#1159](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1159). Settles what [ADR 0085](0085-depth-is-one-axis-over-a-stack-of-tall-dimensions-and-the-dimension-machinery-stays.md) left open ("how seamless the seam must be, and whether the fade and the arrival pocket stay"). Builds on the seam of [ADR 0029](0029-the-campaign-is-one-tall-world-and-the-uncharted-chain-joins-through-seams.md), whose rejection of a fade as the plan still holds.

## Decision

- **The target is a seamless swap.** The server streams the next world's arrival area ahead of the crossing, the client builds a second world from it, and at the seam it switches to that world without vanilla's respawn path. Position, velocity, rotation and interpolation carry over.
- **Today's teleport and fade are the fallback, not the plan.** `BreachService`'s crossing, its fading `BreachPayload` and its carved arrival pocket stay as the path a crossing takes when the swap cannot run: the client has no pre-built world, or the arrival is obstructed.
- **A refusal sends the whole vehicle through the fallback.** The swap is claimed for a vehicle and its riders together. If any rider's client refuses, the whole vehicle crosses by the teleport: it does not bounce back up, and it never splits, with some riders below and some above.
- **The arrival pocket is a safety net.** It is carved only on the fallback path and when the swap's arrival point is obstructed. It is no longer the normal way to arrive.
- **The server streams the arrival area first, in every case.** This is slice 1, and it ships on its own because it also removes the stall the fallback has today: a layer-dimension crossing no longer generates the chunks it lands in on the server thread.
  - **The ticket.** `deepcharter:seam_arrival` is a ticket type registered in `BuiltInRegistries.TICKET_TYPE`: it loads and simulates its chunks and keeps the dimension active, with no `PERSIST` flag, so a restart takes none and the approach starts again. It times out 100 ticks after its last refresh. It is taken with `ServerChunkCache.addTicketAndLoadWithRadius`, which schedules the chunks on the worker threads and returns a future, and refreshed every 20 ticks with `addTicketWithRadius`.
  - **The area.** Radius 4 around the chunk under the root vehicle: 81 chunks, at full status.
  - **Whose approach.** One approach per root vehicle carrying a player, keyed by the root's UUID and listing the riders. A pod with two riders is one approach, not two. The approach has no level or player reference.
  - **The trigger.** The local distance `d` above the level's floor, in blocks, so it needs no global depth ([#1113](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1113)). A vehicle streams when `d` is inside 160 blocks of the floor, or when `d` is inside the lead and its descent speed would cover the lead's distance in the time the design fall speed does. The lead is 0.32 blocks per row of the world below, rounded up: 656 blocks for 2048 rows, 1301 for 4064. The design fall speed is terminal speed, 3.9 blocks a tick (78 a second). The descent speed is read over the last 10 ticks, so a vehicle that sits or climbs streams only inside the 160 blocks.
  - **The last level, the campaign world and the surface.** A level with no level below it takes no ticket, and neither does the campaign world: its layers are bands of one level and nothing is crossed ([ADR 0061](0061-a-crossing-in-the-campaign-world-is-an-event-in-place-found-by-the-root-vehicles-y-with-the-crust-rows-as-hysteresis.md)). The surface floor is not a seam ([ADR 0029](0029-the-campaign-is-one-tall-world-and-the-uncharted-chain-joins-through-seams.md)).
  - **The release.** An approach ends when its vehicle leaves the zone, changes level, is removed or disconnects. The ticket is not removed: it lapses on its timeout, which keeps the arrival area through the swap. A re-centre of a live approach does remove the old centre's ticket. A crossing that finds the area not loaded still works, waits for the chunks on the server thread as before and logs how many of the 81 were loaded.
- **Nothing is sent to the client in slice 1.** The player sees no change. Slice 1 streams the descent only: a rise through a level's top still lands by the fallback crossing, as before.
- **The Sodium outcome.** Sodium needs no mixin of ours: its chunk tracker is per level and its renderer seeds from it when a level is set ([#1183](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1183)). The swap works under Sodium and under vanilla's renderer alike.
- **The crust at a mid-layer seam is open.** Whether a seam that falls inside a layer carries a crust, or none, is with the user. Slice 1 does not depend on it: the trigger reads the distance to the level's floor and nothing about what is there.

## Considered Options

- **(a) A seamless swap alone, with no fallback.** It is the target, but it cannot be the only path. It needs Sodium's per-level `RenderSectionManager` to hold a second world, and mixins on the respawn path (`ClientPacketListener`'s dimension change, the level renderer, sound and the player's interpolation). A crossing then costs about 0.6 MB of chunk data per rider at 256 rows (7.5 KB a column, [#185](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/185)), 4.8 MB at 2048 and 9.5 MB at 4064. Every client without the pre-built world, every refusal and every obstructed arrival would have no way through. Rejected as the sole path.
- **(b) The teleport with its fade as the plan.** Cheap and already built, but it is what ADR 0029 rejected: vanilla's respawn path rebuilds every mesh, stops all sound and freezes the player for about 1.2 seconds, at the speed of a fall of 78 blocks a second. Kept as the fallback only. That rejection still covers it.
- **(c) The arrival pocket as the way to arrive.** A carved room under the ceiling hides the stall, but it cuts the world the player lands in, and the carve needs the chunks loaded on the server thread. Kept as a safety net.
- **Sending the chunks to the client in slice 1.** It would let the client build ahead at once, but it ships that data (0.6 MB per rider at 256 rows, 9.5 MB at 4064) for a swap that does not exist yet, and it cannot be tested without the Sodium spike. Deferred to slice 2.
- **A fixed 600-block lead.** ADR 0029's figure holds for a 256-tall world only. At 0.32 blocks a row a 4064-tall world needs 1301 blocks, and a fixed lead is too short for it and wastes streaming on a short one. Rejected for a lead that scales with the height below.
- **A vanilla ticket type** (`PORTAL` or `ENDER_PEARL`). Their timeouts (300 and 40 ticks) are not ours, and portals and pearls share them: a refresh by one is a refresh of the other. A type of our own keeps the timeout in `LayerTuning` and the ticket findable in a test.

## Slice 2: data ahead

Issue [#1185](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1185). The server sends the arrival area to the riders whose clients have the channels, the client builds the world under the vehicle in the background, and the respawn into that dimension adopts it. The swap without a respawn is slice 3. A client that cannot adopt takes the teleport and the fade, as before.

- **Payloads** (`layer/`, one record each). `seam_prepare` (S2C): the dimension, its type, the obfuscated seed, the sea level, flat, debug and the centre chunk, taken from `ServerPlayer.createCommonSpawnInfo` so they are what the respawn will say. `seam_chunk` (S2C): the dimension and vanilla's own `ClientboundLevelChunkWithLightPacket`, built as `PlayerChunkSender` builds it. `seam_discard` (S2C): the dimension. `seam_missed` (C2S): the dimension. A rider is served only when its client announced `seam_chunk`; one without the mod gets nothing and crosses by the fallback.
- **What is sent.** `SeamSender` runs at the end of each server tick, after the approach is `ready()`. Per rider: a prepare, then the chunks of the area nearest the vehicle first (squared distance, then x, then z), until the tick's estimated bytes reach `seamBytesPerTick` (49 152; the estimate is the packet's block data and 2048 bytes a light array) and at least one a tick. A rider that moves two chunks gets the prepare again and only the chunks it does not hold. A payload over a megabyte is never sent. A rider whose approach ends or who leaves the level gets a discard; a disconnect and a server stop drop the state with none. Built payloads are shared by the riders of a tick.
- **Radius.** The ticket of radius 4 puts its outermost ring at the full status, and `ChunkMap.getChunkToSend` gives only block-ticking chunks, so what is sendable is the inner radius 3: 49 chunks. Vanilla sends the ring after the crossing from the player's own tickets.
- **The client** (`client/layer/SeamClient`). A prepare builds a `ClientLevel` that is not the shown one, with the listener's difficulty and chunk radius; a chunk goes into its chunk cache and its light is queued on it. The light runs with the listener's `level` set to the held level, since Sodium reads that field when light is applied. The held level is drained each client tick and marks nothing dirty in the renderer (`ClientLevelSeamMixin`). It is released on a discard, a disconnect, or a respawn into its dimension that does not adopt it.
- **The crossing.** `BreachService` runs the teleport inside `SeamSender.crossing`. On the server, `ChunkMap.markChunkPendingToSend(ServerPlayer, ChunkPos)` skips a chunk the rider was sent, unless the chunk changed since (`ServerChunkCache.blockChanged` marks it and its eight neighbours stale) or `LightSync` is settling its light, so the pocket and its neighbours are sent again by vanilla. The skipped chunks are kept for `seamMissedWindowTicks` (100). On the client, `ClientPacketListener.handleRespawn` claims the held level when the respawn describes the dimension, type, seed, sea level, flat and debug the prepare did and the player is not dying; its two `new` expressions then return the held level and its data, and the rest of the method runs as it does. A respawn that does not adopt into a dimension the server prepared sends `seam_missed`, and the server sends the skipped chunks again.
- **The fade.** `BreachEffects` holds black at the end of its black stretch until the client reports itself loaded, which follows the section the player is in being meshed, for up to ten seconds. Vanilla's renderer and Sodium's both answer that, so one signal serves both.
- **No forget suppression.** Vanilla sends no forget packet at a change of dimension: the player has been removed from the old level when `PlayerChunkSender.dropChunk` runs, and a removed player is not alive. `SeamDataAheadTest.aCrossingSendsNoForgetAfterTheRespawn` guards that: a forget after the respawn would name a position the adopted level shares with the old one.
- **Mixin targets.** Server: `ChunkMap.markChunkPendingToSend(ServerPlayer, ChunkPos)` at its head; `ServerChunkCache.blockChanged` at its head. Client: `ClientPacketListener.handleRespawn` (after `ensureRunningOnSameThread`, the two `new` expressions, and the return), accessors and invokers on `ClientPacketListener`'s `level`, `serverChunkRadius`, `serverSimulationDistance`, `applyLightData` and `enableChunkLight`, and the four dirty-mark methods of `ClientLevel`. Each has `require 1`: a rename fails loudly.
- **Considered and rejected.** Meshing the held level off screen: it cannot be done without the renderer's own state for a level it does not show (the reasoning is in #1183). Sending a chunk again on every change: it churns without bound under a lava flow or a digging pod, where marking it stale and sending it at the crossing costs one send. A per-chunk acknowledgement: the connection delivers in order, so every chunk is applied before the respawn that follows it.

## Consequences

- **No player-visible change in slice 1.** The campaign world has no seam below it yet. In the layer dimensions, a crossing stops stalling on generation when the player has lingered near the floor.
- **Cost.** One area per approaching root vehicle, never more: the centre moves to the root's chunk only once the root is 2 chunks (Chebyshev) from it, and the old centre's ticket is removed at that moment, so a pod moving sideways at speed holds 81 chunks, not one area per chunk it crossed. N approaching roots cost N times 81 chunks. The ticket simulates its chunks, so mobs of the lower world tick under it. With today's short layers the trigger zone covers most of the level above: layer_1 is 192 tall and the floor margin is 160, so a vehicle streams over most of layer_1. That is accepted. The lead of layer_2 (256 rows, 82 blocks) is under the margin, so the speed branch of the trigger only matters for tall worlds.
- **Registry sync.** `deepcharter:seam_arrival` is a registry entry of a vanilla registry. Fabric's registry sync needs nothing from the mod beyond registering it in `init`: the entry is registered on a client and a server alike, in the same order, and the client never reads it. A client without the mod cannot join anyway, as with the mod's blocks.
- **Slice 3.** Slice 3 is the swap itself and the claim, accept or refuse handshake. A refusal sends the whole vehicle through the fallback, as above.

# Away settling rolls visits in fixed windows of game time from draws surveyed while loaded, and applies them when the outpost's zone is loaded

[ADR 0072](0072-creatures-break-exposed-unrated-blocks-even-while-nobody-is-near.md) decided that creatures keep acting on an outpost nobody is near, and [CREATURES.md](../CREATURES.md#while-nobody-is-near) says how it should feel. Issue [#1004](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1004) builds the part that needs no creature: the ledger, the roll, the apply and the report. This ADR records the shape, so the creature sub-issues ([#1005](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1005), [#1006](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1006)) add draws and rates without changing it.

## Decision

- **A ledger for every outpost, apart from the outposts.** `OutpostVisits` (a versioned SavedData, `deepcharter:outpost_visits`, version 1) holds one `Ledger` for each (charter, layer): `settledTo` (a window number), the last `Survey`, the `Pending` visits and the `Report` the charter has not read. It is separate from `OutpostData`, so a ledger this build cannot read (`Unreadable`, kept and written back) never stops an outpost's terminals. A ledger begins lazily, at the window it is first seen, with no survey, so no visit is ever applied for time before it began.
- **The world's clock, not the day clock.** Time is `getGameTime()`, which stops while the server is off (so a server that is down ruins nothing) and which `/time` cannot move. Windows are `gameTime / windowTicks` (1,200 ticks, a minute). A rate is "per day" of 24,000 game ticks whatever the day clock says.
- **Window-aligned seeds.** Each window rolls each family (Retained, Sleeper's, Recovery) with a random source seeded from `mix(outpostSeed, window, family)`, where `outpostSeed = mix(worldSeed, charter id, layer)`. A window's result never depends on which windows were rolled with it, so one catch-up over `[a, c)` equals `[a, b)` plus `[b, c)`: a sweep may roll any number of windows, and a restart or a slow server changes nothing. At most one visit of a family in a window; the chance is `perDay * windowTicks / 24000`.
- **Draws are surveyed while loaded.** An unloaded outpost cannot be looked at, so what it has to lose is what the last look found: `dark` (block light below the creatures' own lit level at the hall's floor, in the middle and four quadrants), fuel items and ore items in storage, `noise` (the terminals in the zone, a placeholder [#1005](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/1005) refines) and the blocks a Sleeper's visit may break. A family rolls only when it has something to take: Retained need a dark outpost with stored fuel, the Sleeper's need noise and something breakable, Recovery need stored ore.
- **A player near holds the visits.** A player who is not a spectator, in the outpost's level and within 48 blocks of its middle, stops the rolling: the windows pass unrolled. Creatures near a player are the figures' own business, and the player is there to see them. Visits already pending still apply when the zone is loaded.
- **Counts, not targets.** `Pending` stores how many visits wait. What a visit takes is chosen when it is applied, from what is in the zone then, so a charter that emptied its barrels while away loses nothing it no longer has, and nothing stale is kept in the save.
- **Applied when the zone is loaded, never by loading it.** The zone is the hall grown by a reach (8 out, 8 up, 2 down). The sweep applies the pending visits only when every chunk of the zone is loaded (`getChunkNow`); it never forces or loads one. A visit takes a bounded amount from storage block entities, never from an entity: the Retained take a quarter of the stored fuel items, one to sixteen; the Recovery take up to four ore, dearest first. Blocks in `deepcharter:never_settled` (terminals and salvage heaps) are never taken from or broken.
- **A sweep every 100 ticks** over the world's outposts, at the server's tick end. Unreadable outpost or ledger data skips the sweep and never throws. An outpost whose settling throws is logged once and its pending visits are dropped, its windows marked settled, so a deterministic failure costs what the attempt did before the throw, once; that is not in the report.
- **OutpostData version 2.** An outpost now records its dimension, because the sweep must find its level without guessing. Version 1 loads with the dimension of its layer and saves as version 2. That default is right: a version 1 outpost can only exist in a world-format 1 world, where a layer is a dimension of its own, and [ADR 0059](0059-the-campaign-world-has-fixed-bands-from-y-320-to-y-minus-2032-and-old-worlds-are-not-migrated.md) makes #803 refuse every such world. An outpost built in the campaign world stores the overworld, from `level.dimension()`.
- **The salvage heap keeps vanilla's format.** A broken storage block becomes a `salvage_heap` (54 slots, a six-row chest, vanilla `Items` NBT so a pack or a command can read it) holding what the block held and the block's own item. The container is cleared before the block is replaced, or vanilla drops the contents as well. Overflow goes to a heap beside it. Anyone may open a heap; breaking it drops what is left, as a wreck's cargo stays salvageable.
- **Said plainly.** A rolled visit tells the charter's online members "Disturbance reported at Outpost N." (N is the layer, until outposts have numbers of their own); offline members are told nothing then. The first member of the charter to open one of the hall's terminals on return is sent an incident report and the ledger's unread report is cleared.
- **The exposure rule is a stub.** Which blocks a visit can reach is [#926](https://github.com/pkeppeler/deepcharter/issues/926)'s (`OutpostShell.reachable`); until it lands every block of the zone counts as reachable.

## Considered options

- **Roll every tick while unloaded.** Rejected: an unloaded outpost has no tick, and a roll that ran per tick would differ with server load.
- **Store the targets a visit chose.** Rejected: the targets can be gone by the time the zone loads, and the list grows with the time away. Counts bound the save, and the choice at apply time uses the world as it is.
- **Simulate the creatures in unloaded chunks.** Rejected: it needs the chunks loaded, the cost the whole design avoids; a roll from surveyed draws gives the same player-visible result.

## Consequences

- #1005 and #1006 set rates in `SettlingTuning.layers` and refine the draws in the survey; the ledger and the roll need no change. Every built layer's Sleeper's and Recovery rate is 0 until then; the Retained rate is set from the start, so the fuel in a dark outpost's storage is the first thing that can be lost.
- A test pins the number of layers `SettlingTuning` has rates for to the number the world has.
- A charter that keeps its outpost lit, or its fuel in the pod, loses nothing. A dark hall with a full barrel loses it in days of game time (the pacing test prints the table).
